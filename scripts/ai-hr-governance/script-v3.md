# Governing AI in HR — Script v3 (Re-hook Edition)

**Target length:** ~4:15
**Re-hook formula:** every section ends by opening the next loop ("…but the last one makes or breaks you"), and every list teases its final item.

---

**SCENE 0: COLD OPEN (0:00)**

**VO:**
Six months ago, a company switched on an AI tool to help screen job applicants.
Today, a rejected candidate is asking a simple question: why?
And nobody in the building can answer.
Not IT. Not Legal. Not the supplier.
Not even HR.

**ON SCREEN:** The AI tool clicks into a company building and switches "ON". The calendar flips to +6 MONTHS. The candidate asks "Why was I rejected?" The lights in the IT, Legal, Supplier and HR windows switch off one by one. Cut to black: **Nobody knows.**

---

**SCENE 1: THE PROMISE + RE-HOOK (0:25)**

**VO:**
By the end of this video, that same question gets answered in thirty seconds.
It takes three rules.
The first two will keep you out of trouble.
But the third one is the one that makes or breaks most companies.
And almost nobody does it.

**ON SCREEN:** A stopwatch reads **0:30**. Three tiles slam in: **① OWN IT · ② BUY IT RIGHT · ③ ???** The third tile stays locked with a padlock and a glow. The tiles shrink into a tracker that stays on screen.

---

**SCENE 2: RULE 1, OWN IT (0:45)**

**VO:**
Rule one. So who should have answered that candidate?
IT, Legal, Procurement, Data Protection, HR.
Everyone owned a piece.
Nobody owned the outcome.

**ON SCREEN:** Five department icons fly in fast and each grabs a puzzle piece off the tool. The centre is left empty: **OUTCOME: unowned**

**VO:**
And no, a committee doesn't fix this. A committee oversees. It can't answer for every tool, every day.
Every AI tool needs one named owner.
Someone who can answer three questions.
Why does this tool exist?
How is it really being used?
And the one that matters most:
what would tell us it's going wrong?

**ON SCREEN:** An **OWNER** badge drops into the centre. The three questions tick in, and the third one glows larger.

**VO:**
And when it is going wrong, the owner knows who can hit pause.
But here's the catch.
If leadership doesn't set the boundaries, every hard question rolls downhill,
and your managers end up responsible for problems they have no power to fix.

**ON SCREEN:** A ⏸ button pulses. "Hard question" boulders roll down an org chart onto a manager: **Accountable ✓ Authority ✗**

**VO:**
Here's a quick test. Six months after launch, can you still name the owner?
If you can't, your governance is already broken.

**ON SCREEN:** The calendar flips to +6 MONTHS and the badge flickers to **???**

**RE-HOOK VO:**
But an owner can't save a tool that was set up to fail on the day you bought it.
And our company? They thought they'd bought it perfectly.

---

**SCENE 3: RULE 2, BUY IT RIGHT (1:45)**

**VO:**
Rule two.
They checked security. They checked the data. They signed the contract.
The demo looked amazing.
And it still went wrong.

**ON SCREEN:** A checklist ticks green and the demo sparkles. Record scratch, and the sparkle drains away.

**VO:**
Because a great product can't fix a broken process.
Managers used it in ways nobody planned.
And a feature built to sort CVs slowly crept into decisions about people's careers.

**ON SCREEN:** The tool drops into a tangled pipeline. A small "Sort CVs" arrow stretches toward a big **CAREERS** box.

**VO:**
So before you buy, there are six questions to ask your supplier.
They're all on screen. Screenshot them.
But three matter most.
How do you test it?
What happens when you update it?
And the one most buyers forget:
can you explain a result when someone challenges it?

**ON SCREEN:** A grid of six cards: **6 QUESTIONS FOR YOUR SUPPLIER** (hold 3s). The three spoken questions light up in turn, and the last one flashes.

**VO:**
Then get it in writing, because demo promises vanish the moment there's a real problem.
And keep your own off switch.
You buy a system once.
You govern it every day you use it.

**ON SCREEN:** A demo bubble deflates. A contract slides in, and an OFF switch moves into the company's hand. A tiny **BUY** dot is followed by a long **GOVERN** bar.

**RE-HOOK VO:**
So now you've got an owner, and a solid contract.
You're safe, right?
This is exactly where most companies get caught.

---

**SCENE 4: RULE 3, WATCH IT (2:45)**

**VO:**
Rule three. The one that makes or breaks you.
Most governance stops at sign-off. Approved. Next.
But sign-off is exactly when the real evidence starts to arrive.
Before launch, you test what you expect people to do.
After launch, you see what they actually do.

**ON SCREEN:** The padlock on tile ③ breaks: **WATCH IT**. Projects are stamped **APPROVED** and roll away. Split screen: **EXPECTED** shows a straight line, **ACTUAL** shows a tangle.

**VO:**
In our company, managers stopped questioning the tool.
Some staff avoided it. Others used it for things it was never meant to do.
So you watch five things.
Is it accurate?
Is it fair to everyone?
How often do managers override it?
Are complaints building up?
And the one nobody checks:
has it quietly spread beyond what it was approved for?

**ON SCREEN:** A dashboard: **Accuracy · Fairness · Overrides · Complaints · Scope**. The **Scope** widget pulses red.

**RE-HOOK VO:**
But the most dangerous warning sign isn't on the dashboard at all.

**VO:**
Our company's complaints inbox?
Zero. They took that as good news.
But candidates didn't know AI was involved.
Managers blamed themselves, not the tool.
And everyone else assumed you can't argue with the system.
Silence isn't success.
It just means nobody knows they're allowed to ask.

**ON SCREEN:** The inbox shows **0 ✓**. Zoom out to three grey figures with thought bubbles. The tick turns amber: **SILENCE ≠ SUCCESS**

---

**SCENE 5: THE PAYOFF (3:50)**

**VO:**
So, back to that candidate.
Why was I rejected?
This time, the owner picks up.
The contract means the supplier has to help.
And the dashboard flagged the pattern three months ago.
Thirty seconds. Answered.
Own it. Buy it right. Watch it.
Write your rules now, before a problem writes them for you.

**ON SCREEN:** Rewind to the opening. The candidate asks "Why?" and a stopwatch starts. The owner badge, the contract and the dashboard light up in turn. The stopwatch stops at **0:30 ✓**. The three tiles tick ✓, then the end card.
