# Governing AI in HR — Script v2 (Story-Driven, Motion Graphics)

**Source:** https://youtu.be/ds_blTN5YAU (transcript 0:00–6:27)
**Target length:** ~4:45 (~700 words of VO at ~150 wpm)
**Voice:** Calm, authoritative, British English. Short lines, with room to breathe between them (good for TTS too).

### What's different from v1
- **One story runs through the whole video.** A fictional company switches on an AI screening tool, and six months later a rejected candidate asks *why*. Nobody can answer. Each rule rewinds to show where that company went wrong, and the ending replays the story with the rules in place.
- **Problem first, then the fix**, in every section. That gives viewers a reason to keep watching each point.
- **Shorter:** ~4:45 vs ~6:30+. Nothing from the original's substance is lost.
- **Recurring visual cast:** "The Company" (a simple building icon), "The Tool" (a small friendly AI chip character) and "The Candidate". The motion designer animates the same characters throughout instead of inventing new visuals for every line.

## SCENE 0 — Cold open (0:00–0:30)

**VO:**
> Six months ago, a company switched on an AI tool to help screen job applicants.
>
> Today, a rejected candidate is asking a simple question: *why?*
>
> And nobody in the building can answer.
>
> Not IT. Not Legal. Not the supplier.
> Not even HR.

**ON SCREEN:**
- The Company building. The Tool (a small AI chip) clicks into place inside it with a satisfying "ON" switch.
- Calendar flips: **+6 MONTHS**
- The Candidate appears outside with a speech bubble: **"Why was I rejected?"**
- The camera pans across the building's windows: IT, Legal, Supplier, HR. Each light switches off as it's named.
- Hard cut to black. White text: **Nobody knows.**

## SCENE 1 — Setup (0:30–0:45)

**VO:**
> Every organisation using AI will end up with rules for it.
> The only question is whether you write them *before* something goes wrong…
> …or after.
>
> Here are three rules that put you ahead.

**ON SCREEN:**
- A fork in the road: **BEFORE** (clean path) / **AFTER** (path littered with alert icons: a challenged decision, leaked data, a feature nobody knew about).
- Three tiles snap in: **① OWN IT · ② BUY IT RIGHT · ③ WATCH IT**, then shrink to a top-left tracker that stays on screen.

---

## SCENE 2 — Rule 1: OWN IT (0:45–1:55)

**VO:**
> Rule one. Back to our candidate.
> Who *should* have answered them?

**ON SCREEN:** Tracker highlights ①. The Candidate's question bubble floats back in.

**VO:**
> IT built the integration.
> Legal checked the risks.
> Procurement signed the contract.
> Data protection reviewed the personal data.
> And HR runs the hiring process.
>
> Everyone owned a piece.
> Nobody owned the outcome.

**ON SCREEN:**
- The Tool sits in the centre. Five department icons surround it, and each pulls away a puzzle piece as it's named.
- The centre is left empty: **OUTCOME — unowned**

**VO:**
> A governance committee can't fill that gap.
> It can oversee. It can't be accountable for every tool, every day.
>
> So every significant AI tool needs one named owner.
> Not a technical expert, but someone who can answer three questions:
> Why does this tool exist?
> How is it *really* being used?
> And what would tell us it's going wrong?

**ON SCREEN:**
- A committee table icon tries to stretch over a dozen tools and can't reach them all.
- An ID badge drops into the empty centre: **OWNER**
- Three questions tick in beside it.

**VO:**
> The owner also knows who to call:
> who reviews performance, who handles complaints, and who can hit *pause*.

**ON SCREEN:** Three contact chips attach to the badge. A big ⏸ button pulses.

**VO:**
> But owners can't do it alone.
> Leadership has to set the boundaries: how far you'll go with AI, and where you stop.
> Otherwise every hard question rolls downhill,
> and managers end up accountable for problems they have no power to fix.

**ON SCREEN:**
- An executive draws a bold boundary line across the screen.
- Alternate take: "hard question" boulders roll down an org-chart hill and flatten a manager icon. Labels: **Accountable ✓ Authority ✗**

**VO:**
> Quick test.
> Six months from now, the project team has moved on.
> Can you still name the owner?
> If not, your governance has already broken.

**ON SCREEN:** Calendar **+6 MONTHS**. The team walks off-screen and the badge flickers to **???**. Text: **THE 6-MONTH TEST**

## SCENE 3 — Rule 2: BUY IT RIGHT (1:55–3:05)

**VO:**
> Rule two.
> Here's the frustrating part: our company did the buying properly.
> Security, checked. Data handling, checked. Contract, signed.
> The demo looked great.
>
> And it still went wrong.

**ON SCREEN:** Tracker highlights ②. A procurement checklist ticks green, item by item. A demo screen sparkles ✨. Then a record-scratch, and the sparkle drains away.

**VO:**
> Because a great product can't fix a broken process.
> Once it went live, three things happened.
> Managers used it in ways nobody planned.
> The data was solid in one office, and patchy in another.
> And a feature built to sort CVs slowly crept into bigger, higher-stakes decisions.

**ON SCREEN:**
- The Tool slots into a tangled process pipeline, and the output still comes out knotted.
- Three numbered vignettes:
  1. A manager uses The Tool upside-down → **Unplanned use**
  2. An office map, half green, half faded → **Uneven data**
  3. A small "Sort CVs" arrow stretches toward a large "Promotion / Dismissal" box → **Scope creep**

**VO:**
> So don't just ask the supplier "does it work?"
> Ask them:
> How do you test it?
> What data was it built on?
> How do you monitor it?
> What changes when you update it?
> Can you explain a result when someone challenges it?
> And will you help if an unfair pattern shows up?

**ON SCREEN:**
- "Does it work?" gets crossed out.
- Six question cards deal into a 2×3 grid, one per VO line. Header: **6 QUESTIONS FOR YOUR SUPPLIER** (hold 2s as a screenshot-able frame).

**VO:**
> Then put the answers in the contract,
> because demo promises fade fast when there's a real problem.
> Who investigates. Who fixes errors. Who tells you when things change.
> And keep your own off switch, so you're never waiting on the supplier's timetable.

**ON SCREEN:**
- Demo speech bubble deflates. A contract slides in with three highlighted clauses: **Investigate · Correct · Communicate**
- An OFF switch slides from the supplier's side of the screen into the company's hand.

**VO:**
> You buy a system once.
> You govern it every day you use it.

**ON SCREEN:** Timeline: one tiny dot labelled **BUY**, then a long bar labelled **GOVERN** running off the edge of the screen.

## SCENE 4 — Rule 3: WATCH IT (3:05–4:15)

**VO:**
> Rule three, and this is the one most organisations miss.
> Governance usually stops at sign-off.
> Approved. Next project.
>
> But sign-off is exactly when the real evidence starts to arrive.

**ON SCREEN:** Tracker highlights ③. A conveyor belt stamps projects **APPROVED** and they roll away. The governance team turns its back, and behind them warning dots start popping up around The Tool.

**VO:**
> Before launch, you test what you *expect* people to do.
> After launch, you see what they *actually* do.

**ON SCREEN:** Split screen. **EXPECTED:** one neat straight line. **ACTUAL:** a tangle of paths.

**VO:**
> In our company, managers stopped questioning the tool's recommendations.
> Some staff avoided it completely.
> Others found new uses for it, or mixed its results with other information,
> and quietly changed what the outcomes meant.

**ON SCREEN:** The tangled paths get labels one by one: **Over-reliance · Avoidance · New uses · Mixed signals**

**VO:**
> So watch two things: the system, and the people using it.
> Are the results accurate and useful?
> Is anyone being affected differently?
> How often do managers override it?
> Are complaints, corrections or odd outcomes building up?
> And has it spread beyond what it was approved for?

**ON SCREEN:** A dashboard with two panels, **SYSTEM | PEOPLE**. Five widgets animate in: **Accuracy · Fairness · Overrides · Complaints · Scope**

**VO:**
> And the company's complaints inbox?
> Zero. They took that as good news.
>
> But the candidates didn't know AI was involved.
> Managers blamed themselves for bad results, not the tool.
> And everyone else assumed the system couldn't be questioned.
>
> Silence isn't success.
> It might just mean nobody knows they're allowed to ask.

**ON SCREEN:**
- Inbox counter: **0 ✓** in green.
- Zoom out to reveal three grey figures with thought bubbles: "AI was involved?" · "Must be my fault." · "Can't argue with the system."
- The green tick turns amber. Text slams in: **SILENCE ≠ SUCCESS**

## SCENE 5 — Replay (4:15–4:45)

**VO:**
> So let's replay that story.
> A rejected candidate asks: *why?*
>
> This time, there's a named owner who can answer.
> A contract that makes the supplier help investigate.
> And monitoring that spotted the pattern months ago.
>
> Own it. Buy it right. Watch it.
> Write your rules now, before a problem writes them for you.

**ON SCREEN:**
- Rewind effect back to the cold open. The Candidate asks "Why?" again.
- This time the windows light up one by one: the owner badge answers, the contract clause glows, and the dashboard shows the flag raised "3 months ago".
- The Candidate gets a reply bubble ✓.
- The three tiles return full-size and tick ✓ as they're named. Final line types out on screen, then the end card.

## Notes for the motion designer
- **Reuse the cast:** The Company, The Tool, The Candidate. Keep them consistent so the story reads instantly.
- **Colour:** one accent colour per rule, matching the tracker. Amber and red for warnings only.
- **Pacing:** a visual change every 3–5 seconds, and each list item lands on its VO line.
- **On-screen text:** key phrases only. Never put full VO sentences on screen.
